## What this page solves

UTMB qualification is a three-layer system: an eligible UTMB Index category, enough Running Stones, and a Finals lottery entry. A finisher’s medal alone is not a qualification.

The practical test is simple: can you name the exact qualifying race, the exact result, the date it was run and the official page that lists it? If any answer is “I think so”, the entry is not ready.

## Qualification structure

The UTMB World Series has Finals, Majors and Events. Events award Running Stones; Majors award double Stones and automatic Finals places for the top 10 in each distance.

Write the structure on one page: what you must finish, where it must sit in the calendar, and which body confirms it. This prevents a late discovery that a race was never on the list.

## Which races qualify

Only races listed in the current UTMB World Series calendar award Stones. A local trail race, however hard, does not create Stones unless it is in the series.

Choose qualifiers by evidence, not by prestige. Check the official list, the cut-off, the surface and the travel cost; a convenient qualifier you finish beats a famous one you miss.

## Standards and category

UTMB, CCC and OCC are Finals, not open-entry races. In 2026 CCC required a 50K, 100K or 100M Index category, while OCC accepted any category; 2027 may change.

Standards change by age, gender, category or year. Copy the current official table into your own note, with the access date, and delete last year’s version from your phone.

## Entry timeline

The 2026 Finals pre-entry ran 8–19 January and the lottery was on 22 January. Those dates are reference only; the 2027 window is not published.

Build a backward calendar from the race date: qualifier, entry window, document check, travel and taper. Put each deadline in a calendar with a two-week warning.

## Common mistakes

The common errors are treating a finish as a qualification, treating Stones as a ranking, and copying the 2026 January window into a 2027 trip.

Most rejections are administrative, not athletic: wrong race, wrong window, missing proof, missed payment. Read the checklist before blaming fitness.

## From qualification to start line

After selection you still need payment, proof, the Prevention and Health Pathway for adults, and bib collection. The 2026 PPS cost €5 and replaced the medical certificate.

After selection, the work shifts to proof, payment, travel and kit. The start line is not the finish of the qualification process; it is the start of execution.

## Cost, travel and places

Budget entry fee, Chamonix beds, travel from Geneva, Lyon or Milan, mandatory kit, rescue insurance and PPS. Race-week beds are scarce; book a refundable option early.

Put every cost in one sheet: entry, travel, bed, kit, insurance and a buffer. If the total is uncomfortable, choose a closer qualifier rather than borrowing for a race.

## If you miss the cut

If you miss, diagnose the gap: wrong Index category, too few Stones, or lottery luck. Do not buy a transferred bib; UTMB bans transfers and can suspend runners.

If you miss, separate the cause: eligibility, performance, or lottery. Each has a different fix. Repeating the same entry without changing the cause is the expensive path.

## Training and proof

Train for the target category, not just a longer road marathon: weekly climb, technical descent, night head-torch running and long-run fuelling.

Train the demands of the qualifier and the target race, not a generic plan. A qualifier is evidence; the target race is the performance you actually want.

## Qualification matrix

| Item | Position | Status |
|---|---|---|
| UTMB | 100M category | Finals lottery |
| CCC | 50K/100K/100M | Finals lottery |
| OCC | Any Index category | Finals lottery |
| World Series Events | Race distance | Running Stones |
| Majors | 100M/100K/50K | Double Stones + top-10 automatic |

Read the matrix before the narrative. It shows which body confirms each item and which cells are still open. If a cell says TBA, do not fill it with last year’s value.

The matrix is deliberately narrow: it is a checklist, not a training plan. A runner can tick every row and still be unprepared for the race; the reverse is also possible.

## A 12-month checklist

Twelve months out, choose the qualifier and check the official list. Nine months out, build the base and book a refundable room. Six months out, run a tune-up and confirm the entry window. Three months out, submit documents and book travel. Six weeks out, simulate the race. Two weeks out, taper and re-read the rules.

Put every deadline in a calendar with a two-week warning. The most common failure is not fitness but a document that was never uploaded, a payment that was never made, or a race that was never on the list.

## Chinese runner’s perspective

For a runner based in China, the binding constraint is often travel time and visa processing, not the qualifying standard. Start the visa and flight plan as soon as the entry window is known, and keep a refundable bed until the draw.

If the race is in the US or Europe, plan arrival two to three days before packet pickup, not the night before. A delayed flight is a qualification problem only if you let it become one.

## The Index category is the first filter

UTMB's entry system first checks whether you hold the UTMB Index category required for the target race, then Stones and the lottery. The wrong category cannot be rescued by more Stones. The 2026 categories below are 2026 facts only; re-read the official page for 2027.

| Target | 2026 accepted Index category | What it means for scheduling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UTMB | 100M category | At least one 100M-category race |
| CCC | 50K / 100K / 100M | 50K or longer, subject to the year's table |
| OCC | Any UTMB Index category | Short races count, but Stones still needed |
| TDS | Follow the year's rules | Do not copy the CCC table |
| MCC | Follow the year's rules | Do not copy the OCC table |

Ask three questions of every candidate race: does it produce an Index category, how many Stones does it award, and is its distance category accepted by the target? Only three yeses earn a place in the calendar.

## Stones and race selection: few and accurate

Stones are lottery fuel, not a ranking. More races do not bypass the Index threshold; one correctly categorised Major can be more useful than three short races in the wrong category. Write the year as “one Index validation + one or two Stones top-ups”, not “enter everything”.

| Goal | Race combination | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First OCC | One any-category race + one short Stones race | Stacking short races that do not match the category |
| First CCC | One 50K/100K category + one Stones race | Using road marathons instead of trail |
| First UTMB | One 100M category + one Major/100M | Entering Finals without a 100M finish |

Each race has its own entry window; one date does not fit all. The Finals lottery window is not the race-entry window. Keep two calendars so “I entered a race” is not mistaken for “I entered Finals”.
