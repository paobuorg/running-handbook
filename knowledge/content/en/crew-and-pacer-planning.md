## Scope and boundaries

This page covers organizing race-day support: crew (off-course support people) division and handoff points, pacer takeover timing and pace responsibility, segment-by-segment bagging of supplies, and the liaison protocol when contact is lost or the runner withdraws. It applies to trail ultras that admit crew and allow pacers. Road marathons that close support to outsiders, or races that ban pacing, are used only as the contrasting case.

Boundaries first. Access eligibility, pacer age, allowed handoff stations, restricted zones and penalties all follow that edition's official regulations; where the rules do not list a station as open, treat it as closed. This page does not set tactical pace or decide how fast a pacer should run; pace responsibility follows the pacing rules and the runner's state. Medical judgment is outside this page; red-flag symptoms follow the [DNF decision](/en/knowledge/dnf-decision-making/) page.

## Three roles to keep separate: crew, pacer, official pace team

| Role | Position | What they may do | Rule boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crew | Designated aid stations or support areas, off course | Deliver supplies, exchange drop bags, pass information, arrange transport and rest | Usually one person, station-limited, bag-limited; off-course or on-course personal aid may be penalized |
| Pacer | On course, after a designated station | Accompany the runner, assist with fluids and supplies at stations, provide rhythm and psychological support | Often requires registration, waiver and visible badge; carrying supplies and physical pushing are banned |
| Official pace team | On course, public pace groups | Run to a target finish time, usually flying a target-time flag | An event-provided aid to all runners, not a personal pacer; joining is the runner's choice |

Do not mix them: the official pace team serves every runner, a personal pacer serves one runner, and crew do not follow on the course. The 2026 Sydney Marathon pace team page lists target finishes from 2:30 to 7:00 plus a sweep group to 7 hours, each pacer flying the target-time flag; it states the pace groups are an aid to runners, not an official pace requirement.

## Access boundary: read the rules before scheduling

The first if/then step is reading the regulations, not recruiting people.

- **if** the rules list stations open to crew and stations where pacer exchange is allowed; **then** mark each station crew-open / pacer-exchange / neither, as the scheduling base table.
- **if** the rules give general terms without station details; **then** plan conservatively: send no crew to unlisted stations until that edition's guide or on-site announcement confirms.
- **if** the rules bar non-participants from accompanying along the course; **then** arrange no pacer and no friends or family following on course.

Verified boundary examples: UTMB Mont-Blanc rules allow personal assistance at designated aid stations only, one person, with a bag no larger than 30 litres; the runner's own team may not provide medical assistance; OCC, MCC and ETC allow no assistance anywhere on course; pacing or accompaniment by a non-entrant outside marked spectator areas draws a penalty. Big's Backyard Ultra rules bar personal aid during a loop, exclude non-competitors (including eliminated runners) from the course, and allow no pacing; personal support happens only between loops. Boston Marathon rules state a runner may not run with anyone not registered as an official participant, and bibs are non-transferable.

## Crew division card

For a minimum support team, write duties on a card before the race, one per person, so division does not improvise on course.

| Duty | Owner | Pre-race preparation | On-course action | Stop rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lead liaison | One person | Holds runner bib, race hotline, medical and pickup locations | Timing contact, records arrival and departure, passes messages | After withdrawal, notifies family and closes out |
| Aid-station lead | One person (may double) | Bags by segment, checks station hours and parking | Takes position early, hands over by checklist, keeps station entrance clear | Leaves if station is closed; does not seek unofficial access |
| Transport | One person | Checks routes, parking limits and inter-station drive times | Schedules around predicted arrival; no reckless driving or illegal stops | Use official shuttles when no legal parking exists |
| Supply keeper | One person (may double) | Inventory of drop bags, spare clothing, supplies and first-aid items | Holds and distributes, logs what is taken | Carries items when storage is banned; leaves nothing behind |

The card must name a single lead liaison: if contact is lost, all outside communication runs through one voice, avoiding repeated calls and conflicting messages. Western States rules illustrate the detail: at some stations only one crew vehicle may enter, and some are reachable by shuttle only. Hardrock limits crew generally to a 400-yard radius around the aid station, and one crew pass allows one vehicle to park at designated points.

## Handoff points and arrival timing

A handoff point must satisfy three overlaps: open to the team by rules, reachable by the runner, and legally reachable by the team.

- **if** a station admits both crew and pacer exchange; **then** use it as the pacer takeover or change point, with the team arriving before the predicted runner arrival.
- **if** a stretch has neither crew nor pacer exchange; **then** mark that "no-support interval" in the plan, and the runner carries all water, food and clothing for it.
- **if** a station is reachable only by official shuttle; **then** the team schedules around shuttle times and does not try to drive in.

The team should arrive before the predicted runner arrival, leaving time to find a position, park and queue; the handoff itself should be short, with no long stay at the entrance. Under Western States 2026 rules, pacers may start from Foresthill (mile 62); a runner leaving Michigan Bluff (mile 56) after 8:00 p.m. may have a pacer from that point; pacer changes are allowed only at Foresthill, the Rucky Chucky near side, Green Gate, Pointed Rocks and Robie Point. Under Hardrock 2027 rules, pacers are allowed from Grouse Gulch onward and may meet the runner only at crew-access stations — Ouray, Telluride, Chapman — and KT if hiked in, for exchange only; runners over 60 may have pacers for the whole run.

## Supply packing: bags by segment

Pack supplies by segment rather than stuffing everything into one bag.

1. **Group by station**: one bag per open station, labeled with station name, predicted arrival and contents checklist.
2. **Allocate by segment need**: distance, climb, weather and stay time decide fluids, carbohydrate, electrolytes and spare clothing; never introduce a new food or drink on race day.
3. **Pack within rules**: fit the allowed bag type and opening size; UTMB-series rules cap the personal assistance bag at 30 litres, and sealed bags or packaging that cannot be checked immediately are forbidden.
4. **Log what is taken**: record items handed over and removed at stations, so leftovers can be inventoried after withdrawal.
5. **Keep redundancy separate**: spare batteries, rain shell, insulation and first-aid items must be immediately identifiable, not mixed with daily fuel.

The food and fluid rhythm principles are in [race-day fueling](/en/knowledge/race-day-fueling/); trail gear and mandatory items are in [trail gear](/en/knowledge/trail-gear/).

## Pacer takeover timing and pace responsibility

A pacer does not run for the runner; responsibilities are agreed before takeover.

- **Registration and badge**: if the race requires pacer registration, a waiver and a badge; the pacer completes the formalities before the race and wears the badge on course. Western States requires each pacer to pick up a pacer number and sign a release at Pacer Central; three matching numbers are issued per entrant; pacers must be at least 18, with exceptions requested in advance from the Race Director.
- **Takeover moment**: if rules limit the pacer to a station onward; then do not meet earlier on the course or at an earlier station. Hardrock requires the pacer to run at least the whole leg to the next crew-access or designated station.
- **Pace responsibility**: the pacer follows the runner's rhythm and does not force the pace; when the runner slides, the pacer's job is to remind them to eat and drink and hold a safe rhythm, not to push forward.
- **Prohibitions**: most rules forbid carrying the runner's water, food, headlamp, shoes or clothing (muling) and physical assistance on course. Western States allows the pacer to help fill bottles or replenish at aid stations, but not to enter ahead of the runner or depart after the runner to speed refueling; pacers may accept aid at aid stations.
- **Pacer after withdrawal**: if the runner withdraws; the pacer stays at that aid station until another runner requests services, and may not continue on trail without an official entrant.

## Lost-contact and withdrawal liaison protocol

Agree on a liaison flow before race day; improvising in the moment is unreliable.

| Situation | Condition (if) | Action (then) | Stop rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal arrival | Runner reaches station on predicted time | Lead liaison records arrival and departure, updates next prediction | No repeated urging calls |
| Delay | Runner not at station after a buffer window past predicted arrival | Lead liaison first checks race timing/hotline for the point, then alerts the team | Do not drive onto the course to search |
| Suspected lost contact | No timing point for a long stretch, phone unreachable | Report bib and last point to race officials, wait at the designated meeting point on instructions | Do not split into a private search party |
| Runner withdraws | Runner or medical staff reports withdrawal | Record withdrawal station, transport and destination; notify family; arrange pickup | Stop finishing-oriented scheduling once the record is confirmed |
| Pacer steps down | Pacer exchange or ends accompaniment | Log pacer departure station and time, return badge | No continued course presence without an official entrant |

General course-safety and position-description principles are in [route safety](/en/knowledge/route-safety/); the organizational view of medical points, evacuation routes and the race medical plan follows the World Athletics competition medical guidelines cited at the end.

## Three failure modes and early signals

| Failure mode | Early signal | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduling from memory instead of the access list | Scheduled stations are not on the official open list; parking spots are guessed | Team arrives to refusal; fuel and handoff fall through |
| Pacer meets early or mules supplies | Pacer appears before the open station; pack carries runner's fuel and gear | Penalty triggered, disqualification in serious cases |
| Split liaison voice | Several people call at once, messages disagree; no lead liaison | Chaos during lost contact, duplicate rescue requests, missed official guidance |

Matching actions: verify the access list into the schedule before the race; run pacer formalities and exchange points by the rules; fix one lead liaison and an escalation order before start. The overall long-distance rhythm and segment framework are in [ultra-running basics](/en/knowledge/ultra-running-basics/).

## Scope and items the official source does not publish

This page does not represent any edition's actual access arrangements; station openness, pacer eligibility, bag dimensions, parking limits and penalty details may change year to year. The following are marked unknown on this site: next-edition station access lists; crew-reachable locations where rules do not list them; pacer eligibility approvals; private transport routes between stations. Until that edition's guide is obtained, do not treat last year's support plan as this year's rules. This page does not diagnose, prescribe training plans or recommend medication.

## Drop-bag packing: by station, not by category

The common drop-bag mistake is one bag for everything, searched through at the station. Pack by station and time window; each bag holds only what that segment needs, with station, kilometre, name and bib on the outside.

| Station type | Must include | Optional | Do not include |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daytime middle | Carbs, salt, spare gels, sunscreen | Spare socks, buff | Night insulation |
| Evening | Headlamp, batteries, warm mid-layer, long sleeves | Hot food, ginger tea | Extra water |
| Night | Primary + backup light, gloves, hat, insulation | Caffeine, hot drink | Daytime sunglasses |
| Finish | Dry clothes, shoes, phone charger, food | Wash kit | Race kit |

Tape a list to each bag: what to take, what to leave. Crew executes the list and does not add “what he might need”; improvisation adds weight and confusion.

## Three handoff failures

**Scenario 1: runner arrives 40 minutes early.** Crew is still parking or queuing, the runner cannot find them, and mood and pace unravel. Fix: arrive 60 minutes early and agree a written “if I am not here, go to the next point” plan; the runner waits no more than 10 minutes.

**Scenario 2: runner wants a shoe or bag change.** Crew did not bring a spare or cannot find it. Fix: put a broken-in spare shoe and dry socks in the drop bag; keep the change under 10 minutes so the runner does not chill.

**Scenario 3: pacer takes over at the wrong point.** Rules allow pacing only at specific stations and the crew misreads the kilometre. Fix: print the official pacer rules for the car and mark allowed takeover points; the pacer arrives 30 minutes early instead of “meeting on the trail”.

## Parking, road closures and communication

Mountain races have closures, one-way control and signal dead zones. Read the official transport page, mark parking, walking time to the station and a backup road for every access point; “navigation says 10 minutes” is not “10 minutes on race day”. Crew use shared location and a text template (“at station / left / runner status”) so phone lines stay free.
