## Scope and the two scenarios

This page covers the execution flow from waking to the gun and the first 5 km: actions triggered by conditions, with stop rules stated. It splits into two scenarios: road marathons (closed roads, dense crowds, fixed aid stations) and trail races (mountain or footpath terrain, mandatory gear, long station intervals). The shared principle: no new food, gear or fuel on race morning; resolve anything uncertain the day before.

Boundary: this page does not replace that edition's registration, bag-check and wave arrangements; the actual time windows follow the race handbook. Medical discomfort and emergency signals follow the [DNF decision](/en/knowledge/dnf-decision-making/) page; race-day fuel rhythm is in [race-day fueling](/en/knowledge/race-day-fueling/).

## The night before and after waking

- **if** sleep was short the night before or nerves kept you awake; **then** do not treat morning extra sleep as the main recovery; rise on schedule and avoid drinks you do not normally take. General sleep and recovery principles are in [sleep and recovery](/en/knowledge/sleep-recovery/).
- **if** you wake with clear diarrhea, dizziness or stomach pain; **then** take small fluids and observe for 15–20 minutes; if it does not settle, contact on-site medical rather than forcing yourself to the start.
- **if** weather differs from the forecast (rain, wind, cold); **then** change clothing and protection under the backup plan before leaving, instead of adjusting at the start.
- **if** bib, chip or gear was not sorted the night before; **then** check them item by item from a fixed checklist; missing items go to race services, not a last-minute bag scramble before start.

## The eating window three hours before the start

The pre-race breakfast tops up glycogen and avoids an empty stomach without leaving the gut digesting at the gun. The ACSM and Dietitians of Canada practice guideline states: eat 1–4 g of carbohydrate per kg of body weight in the 1–4 hours before exercise, choosing low-fat, low-fibre foods to reduce gastrointestinal problems and promote gastric emptying; the pre-event meal should provide at least about 1 g of carbohydrate per kg to compensate for increased carbohydrate oxidation. Portions are adjusted to body weight, duration, environment and personal tolerance; the range is not a fixed prescription.

- **if** the start is early and less than three hours after waking; **then** choose a small, easy-to-digest carbohydrate meal and do not add high-fat, high-fibre foods.
- **if** a food type upset you in training; **then** avoid it on race day and do not trial a new gel or drink that morning.
- **if** appetite is low in the morning; **then** eat in portions, start with liquid carbohydrate and add solids later, rather than forcing a single large intake.
- **if** it is a long event with a staged fuel plan; **then** morning fuel only covers the opening, with later carbohydrate and electrolyte supply per segment, see [hydration](/en/knowledge/hydration/) and [gut training](/en/knowledge/gut-training-for-races/).

The night-before carbohydrate reserve principle is in [carbohydrate loading](/en/knowledge/carb-loading/); how breakfast connects with daily nutrition is in the pre-/post-run fueling page.

## Arrival, registration and bag check

Arrival time must leave room for registration, bag check, toilets and walking to the corral, rather than reaching right at the gun. NYRR race procedures recommend arriving at least 60 minutes before the start to collect items, check bags, use toilets and enter the starting area; they recommend entering the starting corral at least 10 minutes before the start. New York City Marathon waves and corrals are assigned from the estimated finish time submitted at registration; the 2026 corral closing times were 08:45, 09:25, 10:00, 10:40 and 11:15.

- **if** bag check requires pre-booking or closes the day before; **then** complete it the day before, leaving no bag task for the morning. NYRR NYC Marathon bag check must be selected in advance; pre-bag check closes at 17:00 the day before the race; bags are collected after the finish on Central Park West at 73rd–75th Streets until 19:00, then moved to the NYRR RUNCENTER.
- **if** transport may be delayed; **then** arrive on an earlier service and keep a backup route on the phone.
- **if** the corral is not open on arrival; **then** finish toilets, bag check and warm-up first, then enter when directed, without crossing barriers.
- **if** the bag point cannot be found or the chip misbehaves; **then** handle it at the race service desk, keeping bib and receipt; do not change waves on your own.

## Warm-up, toilet queues and gear check

- **Warm-up**: if it is cold or you sat in transport a long time; then start with easy walking and dynamic stretching to raise heart rate gradually, without a high-intensity surge that spends energy early.
- **Toilets**: if the start line queue is long; then use the stop at your transport or hotel first, and take a second turn at the venue; do not leave it to the queue tail near the gun.
- **Gear check**: if a road race; then check bib, chip, shoes, clothing and carried supplies; if a trail race; then check water, food, insulation, headlamp, rain shell and first-aid items against the mandatory gear list, see [trail gear](/en/knowledge/trail-gear/).
- **Body check**: if chest tightness, dizziness, abnormal palpitations or persistent stomach pain appear before the start; then stop entering the corral and follow the medical pathway, see [cardiac emergency](/en/knowledge/cardiac-emergency-in-running/) and [DNF decision](/en/knowledge/dnf-decision-making/).

## Fifteen minutes before the gun

The last quarter hour is only for finishing touches, not new decisions.

1. Stand in your assigned wave and row, keeping distance around you; do not push into an earlier batch.
2. Take a small fluid rinse, not a large drink; the fluid goal follows the ACSM exercise-hydration basis: prevent dehydration beyond 2% of body weight, with intake adjusted to personal sweat rate.
3. Re-check carried supplies, bib and watch; record the planned pace and target splits on the watch or bib back.
4. Take a few breaths, loosen legs, wait for the gun; do not argue over position or jump the start.

## The pace-braking rule for the first 5 km

The opening kilometres are easiest to rush with the crowd, spectators and excitement. Analysis of split times from a large cohort of Boston Marathon finishers found even pacing associated with the fastest finishes; strong positive splitting (fast early, slow late) was markedly slower, and variable pacing had the highest wall incidence; sub-3:00 runners paced evenly far more often than casual participants. The final morning rule is therefore to hold pace down over the first 5 km.

- **if** the first km pace is faster than target; **then** relax stride and rhythm immediately back to the target range, and do not treat the fast opening as a fitness gain.
- **if** crowd congestion prevents target pace; **then** move through the congestion at passing pace and return to target pace after it clears, without weaving aggressively.
- **if** feel is heavy over the first 5 km; **then** first check eating, fluids and sleep before lowering the overall plan; do not chase time early.
- **Stop rule**: worsening pain, dizziness or unrelieved breathlessness follows the medical pathway; the pace plan yields to body state.

The full framework for pace bands and wall prevention is in [race-day pacing](/en/knowledge/race-day-pacing/); cut-off buffer and segment deadline review are in [cut-off management](/en/knowledge/cutoff-management/).

## Road marathon timeline (general assumptions)

The table is a general assumed framework; clock times follow that edition's regulations. The example is laid out for a morning road marathon.

| Before gun | Action | if/then point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Night before | Bag booking, gear sort, early sleep | Missing the bag deadline leaves no bag in the morning |
| 0–1 h after waking | Pre-race breakfast, small fluid portions | No new foods; avoid high fat and fibre |
| 60 min before | Registration, bag check, toilets | Arrive at least 60 min early (NYRR basis) |
| 10–30 min before | Dynamic warm-up, walk to corral | Waves assigned by predicted finish |
| 15 min before | Enter corral, rinse, gear check | No large drink, no position rush |
| Gun | Cross start evenly, read first km pace | Do not surge with the crowd |
| First 5 km | Hold to target range, watch feel | Relax back to range if faster than target |

## Trail race timeline (differences)

The extra trail-race steps concentrate on gear check, bottle filling and preparation for long station intervals; the main timeline matches the road version, with the differences below.

| Before gun | Extra trail action | if/then point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Night before | Pack mandatory gear, check headlamp battery, review route segments | Missing gear is hard to replace at mountain stations |
| On arrival | Fill water, portion food, count mandatory items | Tick each item off that edition's list |
| Warm-up | Add or remove layers with the weather | Keep insulation until the last moment in cold or wind |
| 15 min before | Confirm start wave, liaison plan and first support point | Carry water and food for the long interval |
| First 5 km | Hold pace for surface and gradient | Do not run trail sections at road pace |

Station access, pacer exchange and support rhythm are in [crew and pacer planning](/en/knowledge/crew-and-pacer-planning/); the long-distance segment framework is in [ultra-running basics](/en/knowledge/ultra-running-basics/).

## Exception cases if/then

| Exception | Condition (if) | Action (then) | Stop rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Late arrival | Arrived past the corral-close reserve | Ask staff about a later starting batch; do not cross barriers | Do not force onto course without registration |
| Bag problem | Booking invalid or bag lost | Register at the service desk; carry only essentials | Do not miss the gun looking for the bag |
| Stomach upset | Morning nausea, diarrhea | Small fluids and observe; medical point if unresolved | Do not enter the start if symptoms worsen |
| Missing gear | Mandatory item left behind | Buy or borrow through race channels; do not start a trail leg short | Do not take the trail route with missing items |
| Weather change | Rain, wind, cold | Change to backup clothing; adjust warmth and grip | Do not tough it out in wet clothing |

## Three failure modes and early signals

| Failure mode | Early signal | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New food or gear on race morning | Swapping breakfast brand or shoes the day before | Gut upset or blisters after start, forcing slowdown or withdrawal |
| Arriving at the gun | No reserve for registration, bag check, toilets | Gear missed in the scramble; corral closed |
| Dragged fast over first 5 km | First km pace well over target, heart rate high | Mid-race buffer spent early; wall or cut-off pressure |

Matching actions: validate everything new on training days; plan for a 60-minute early arrival; hold pace down over the first 5 km. The pre-race checklist can be paired with the [race checklist tool](/en/tools/race-checklist/).

## Scope and items the official source does not publish

This timeline is a general framework and does not represent any edition's specific arrangements. The following are marked unknown on this site: that edition's gun time, corral closing times, bag-check deadline and pickup window; toilet counts and queue lengths at each point; transport schedules between start and finish.
