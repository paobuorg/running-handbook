## What this page solves

Boston qualification is three steps: run a BQ on a certified course, enter in the window, and be accepted. A BQ is an entry standard, not a bib.

The practical test is simple: can you name the exact qualifying race, the exact result, the date it was run and the official page that lists it? If any answer is “I think so”, the entry is not ready.

## Qualification structure

The BAA sets age-group and gender standards. The 2027/2028 18–34 standards are 2:55:00 for men, 3:25:00 for women and 3:25:00 for non-binary runners.

Write the structure on one page: what you must finish, where it must sit in the calendar, and which body confirms it. This prevents a late discovery that a race was never on the list.

## Which races qualify

The time must come from a BAA-certified course with verifiable distance and timing. A training run, virtual run or self-measured 42.195 km is not a BQ.

Choose qualifiers by evidence, not by prestige. Check the official list, the cut-off, the surface and the travel cost; a convenient qualifier you finish beats a famous one you miss.

## Standards and category

There is no single “qualifying time” for all ages. The table steps down by age group, and the full table on the BAA page is the authority.

Standards change by age, gender, category or year. Copy the current official table into your own note, with the access date, and delete last year’s version from your phone.

## Entry timeline

The 2027 race is 19 April 2027 and 2028 is 17 April 2028. The entry window is announced each year; do not copy last year’s dates or assume a late submission.

Build a backward calendar from the race date: qualifier, entry window, document check, travel and taper. Put each deadline in a calendar with a two-week warning.

## Common mistakes

The common errors are treating a BQ as acceptance, using an uncertified downhill course and treating charity entries as a backup BQ. Each has its own rules.

Most rejections are administrative, not athletic: wrong race, wrong window, missing proof, missed payment. Read the checklist before blaming fitness.

## From qualification to start line

After acceptance you submit proof, pay, choose a wave and use the official buses from Boston Common. The course is point-to-point from Hopkinton to Boylston Street.

After selection, the work shifts to proof, payment, travel and kit. The start line is not the finish of the qualification process; it is the start of execution.

## Cost, travel and places

Budget entry, Boston beds, flights, race-day transport and, for charity, the fundraising commitment. Race-week hotels are expensive; book a refundable rate early.

Put every cost in one sheet: entry, travel, bed, kit, insurance and a buffer. If the total is uncomfortable, choose a closer qualifier rather than borrowing for a race.

## If you miss the cut

If you miss the time, choose a certified course and build a 12–16 week plan. If you hit the BQ but miss the cut, that is a place competition, not an invalid time.

If you miss, separate the cause: eligibility, performance, or lottery. Each has a different fix. Repeating the same entry without changing the cause is the expensive path.

## Training and proof

Train to run the target pace on a certified course, not just to run intervals. Use the course profile, altitude and weather; downhill speed does not guarantee certification.

Train the demands of the qualifier and the target race, not a generic plan. A qualifier is evidence; the target race is the performance you actually want.

## A 12-month checklist

Twelve months out, choose the qualifier and check the official list. Nine months out, build the base and book a refundable room. Six months out, run a tune-up and confirm the entry window. Three months out, submit documents and book travel. Six weeks out, simulate the race. Two weeks out, taper and re-read the rules.

Put every deadline in a calendar with a two-week warning. The most common failure is not fitness but a document that was never uploaded, a payment that was never made, or a race that was never on the list.

## Chinese runner’s perspective

For a runner based in China, the binding constraint is often travel time and visa processing, not the qualifying standard. Start the visa and flight plan as soon as the entry window is known, and keep a refundable bed until the draw.

If the race is in the US or Europe, plan arrival two to three days before packet pickup, not the night before. A delayed flight is a qualification problem only if you let it become one.

## Result certificate checklist

| Item | What to verify | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Course | A certified course on the BAA list | Training run, virtual run, uncertified route |
| Distance | 42.195 km with timing and splits | GPS trace assembled to distance |
| Date | Within the valid period before the window | Result older than BAA allows |
| Name | Matches passport and entry | Certificate in English, passport in Chinese |
| Group | Race-day age and category correct | Using entry-day age |
| Submission | Uploaded in window, fee paid | Saved as a draft |

A result certificate is not a screenshot. BAA may require the official race results page or timing-company proof; store links, PDFs and race contacts in advance.

## Admission is not a pass mark on BQ

Meeting BQ only enters the pool. BAA admits by age-group places and fastest results; the actual cut-off changes with applicant numbers, and this page gives no 2027 forecast. What you control is a buffer above the standard and a clean submission, not guessing the cut-off.

If your result is exactly on the line, choose another fast, weather-stable, affordable qualifier rather than treating “just BQ” as guaranteed admission.
