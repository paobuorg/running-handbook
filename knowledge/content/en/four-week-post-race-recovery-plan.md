## What this page solves

The four weeks after a race invite two mistakes: “finally done, do nothing” and “I feel good, train now”. The first turns soreness and fatigue into a chronic problem; the second brings the next injury in week three. Recovery is not four weeks off; it is a weekly dose of different stimulus.

This page gives an executable four-week table: 0–72 hours, week 1, week 2, week 3, week 4, plus nutrition, sleep, medical red flags and return tests. It works for road and trail; for hundred-milers or multi-day events, stretch each stage by 1–2 weeks.

## 0–72 hours: stop the bleeding

After the finish do four things: rehydrate with electrolytes (not plain water in bulk), carbohydrate plus protein, elevate/compress the legs, sleep. No deep-tissue massage immediately, no very hot bath, no celebratory drinking to dehydration.

Chest pain, fainting, confusion, inability to stand, dark urine or persistent vomiting means medical care. In the first 24 hours these matter more than placing.

## Week 1: walking only

No running in week 1. Walk easily 20–40 minutes daily, split into 1–2 sessions; add two 10-minute mobility/glute activations. If pain or gait worsens while walking, cut to 10 minutes or rest.

Sleep first: add 30–60 minutes per night. Keep protein at 1.6–2.0 g/kg and do not crash carbohydrate. Do not use “recovery week” as a binge excuse, and do not fast to “clean the body”.

## Week 2: walk-run

Week 2 adds walk-run: 2 minutes walk / 1 minute easy jog for 20–30 minutes, every other day. Keep the effort conversational; any pain means walk. 

Strength is low load only: glute bridge, calf raise, dead bug, banded side-step. No heavy squat/deadlift and no “compensation training” in a recovery week.

## Week 3: continuous easy jogging

Week 3 targets one continuous 30-minute easy jog, 60–90 seconds/km slower than race pace, three times, every other day. Weekly volume no more than 50% of pre-race volume.

Add one session of 4–6 × 20-second strides (not sprints) to check symmetry. If left-right difference or knee/Achilles heat appears, return to week 2.

## Week 4: back to eighty percent

Week 4 can return to 70–80% of pre-race weekly volume with one full rest day. One light quality session (e.g. 6 × 1 minute threshold or 4 × 2 minute hills), the rest easy.

For a 100 km/100 mile race, week 4 stays at 50–60%; do not force this table. Recovery has no “behind”, only “redo”.

## Nutrition and fluids

Spread protein over four meals at 0.3–0.4 g/kg each; adjust carbohydrate to training and avoid chronic low-carb. Add vegetables and fruit, plus potassium/magnesium foods (banana, potato, leafy greens, nuts).

For 24 hours after the race, eat 10–20% above normal, then return to balance. Do not punish yourself with the scale; glycogen and water can swing weight by 1–2 kg.

## Sleep

Sleep is the master switch. For 1–2 weeks after the race, aim for 8–9 hours; if you wake repeatedly, fix wake time, get daylight, cut evening screens and caffeine.

Do not replace daily sleep with weekend catch-up. Persistent snoring, apnoea or daytime sleepiness needs assessment; that is a health issue, not a training one.

## Medical red flags

See a clinician for: pain at rest at night, morning stiffness over 30 minutes, unilateral swelling and heat, arch/heel tenderness lasting a week, abnormal urine, palpitations or persistent low fever.

Do not mask pain with analgesics to run; NSAIDs can add risk under dehydration/renal stress. With a history of injury, get a review before loading.

## Chinese runners and 40+

Many Chinese races are on Sunday and work resumes Monday. Split week-1 walking into commute plus a 10-minute lunch walk; do not force one 40-minute block.

Recovery is slower over 40: tendon/fascia perfusion and repair decline, so the week-3 “continuous 30 minutes” can move to week 4. Use morning resting heart rate, sleep and pain, not the calendar.

## Common mistakes

Mistake one: deep massage or painful stretching on race day. Two: “flush run” in week 1. Three: running through pain with analgesics. Four: heavy lifting in a recovery week. Five: returning to pre-race intensity in week 4.

Six: watching pace but not gait and pain. Seven: adding training on insufficient sleep. Each turns a four-week recovery into an eight-week rehab.

## Four-week table

| Week | Running | Strength/mobility | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–72 h | None | Elevate/light movement | Fluids, sleep |
| Week 1 | Walk only | 2 light sessions | Pain-free walking |
| Week 2 | Walk-run | 2 low-load | 20–30 minutes |
| Week 3 | 3 easy jogs | 2 low-load | 30 minutes pain-free |
| Week 4 | 70–80% volume | 2 sessions | One light quality |

## Checklist

Daily: sleep, pain 0–10, gait symmetry, morning resting heart rate. Weekly: volume within cap, any new pain, strength completed.

Return test: 30 minutes pain-free, no next-day swelling, pain-free descending stairs, 20 single-leg calf raises symmetrical. All four before week 4.

## Four recovery metrics

Do not judge recovery by feel alone. Track four numbers daily: morning resting heart rate (more than 7 beats above baseline means cut back), sleep duration and quality, pain 0–10, gait symmetry. If two of four worsen, step back a week.

Female runners should also track cycle and energy availability; chronic low availability slows recovery and affects bone density. Do not treat weight loss as proof of good training.

## What counts as recovered

Recovered is not “no pain”; it is: 30 minutes easy running pain-free, no next-day swelling, pain-free down stairs, 20 symmetrical single-leg calf raises, sleep and resting heart rate back to baseline. All five before quality work.

If you only want to “run again”, pass at least the first three. Do not prove recovery with a race result; judge by the 24-hour response to training.

## The first session back

Choose flat ground, good weather and a charged phone. Warm up with 10 minutes walking and dynamic drills, run easy 20–30 minutes, cool down with 5 minutes walking. Conversational the whole way; ignore pace.

Record 24 hours later: did pain return to zero, was gait normal, did sleep worsen. If pain rises above 3/10 the next day, halve the next session; two rises in a row means back to walking.
