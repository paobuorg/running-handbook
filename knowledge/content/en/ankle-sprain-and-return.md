## When to use this protocol

This protocol is for runners with a confirmed **lateral ankle sprain** and no signs of fracture or high ankle injury. It arranges the graded return from the acute stage to running, jumping and cutting. It answers "which level am I allowed in now, and where do I step back if stuck"; it does not replace diagnosis, grading or imaging decisions by a doctor or physiotherapist.

It does not apply if you heard a crack at injury, the joint is deformed or at an odd angle, the toes are numb, cold or discoloured, you cannot take four steps, or the ankle remains unstable; those need emergency or orthopaedic assessment first, not a self-guided pass through these levels.

## If/then decision table

| If this applies | Band | First action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh sprain, pain and swelling marked, but short-distance weight-bearing is possible with protection; no deformity, numbness or coldness | Level 0 acute protection | Protect briefly, control swelling, check care signals the same day |
| Can walk on flat ground with a near-normal gait; swelling and pain ease day by day | Level 1 weight-bearing and range of motion | Pain-free range-of-motion exercises, gradual weight-bearing |
| Flat walking pain-free; range of motion close to the uninjured side | Level 2 strength and balance | Heel raises, isometric work and proprioception, compared side to side |
| Heel raises and single-leg stance pain-free and near-symmetric | Level 3 hopping and cutting | Two-foot hops, then single-leg hops, then agility cuts, step by step |
| Hopping and cutting pain-free; one full training session completed | Level 4 running and sport-specific return | Start with straight easy jogging, build volume, then return to race movements |
| Swelling rebounds, pain worsens, or the ankle gives way at any level | Step back one level; seek care if it does not settle | Stop the current level, return to pain-free load |

## Level checklist: entry conditions, stay criteria, failure step-backs

The level order follows the AAOS three-phase rehabilitation, the BJSM 2018 ankle sprain clinical guideline, and the PAASS return-to-sport consensus. Fixed counts for heel raises and hops are not set by an official threshold; the counts below are example starting points, capped by "no worsening the next day and no clear side-to-side gap."

| Level | Entry conditions | Stay criteria (pass before next level) | Failure step-back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Level 0 acute (roughly first 1–3 days) | Fresh sprain; red flags first excluded | Swelling and pain begin to settle; short protected weight-bearing possible; circulation and sensation in the foot normal | Cannot bear weight, marked swelling or deformity, numbness, cold or discolouration, instability after a crack — seek care immediately, do not proceed |
| Level 1 weight-bearing and range of motion | No red flags; protected short-distance weight-bearing possible | Normal flat-ground gait without crutches; ankle dorsiflexion/plantarflexion and inversion/eversion within pain-free range; swelling no longer rising | Pain or swelling rebounds after weight-bearing, or gait limps — return to Level 0; inability to bear weight after 4–5 days needs clinical examination |
| Level 2 strength and balance | Level 1 passed; walking pain-free | Single-leg heel raises pain-free with near-matching counts on both sides (example start: 3 sets of 10, chase no number); single-leg stance stable without obvious sway on eyes-closed or soft surface; calf and outer-hip strength recovered | Heel raises hurt, soreness worsens next day, or single-leg stance sways clearly — return to Level 1 with reduced exercise; recurrent instability needs physiotherapy assessment |
| Level 3 hopping and cutting | Level 2 passed; heel raises and balance symmetric | Small two-foot jumps pain-free; single-leg hop distance and landing stability near-symmetric; zigzag cuts and agility drills pain-free without giving way | Landing pain on hops, ankle rolls inward on cutting, next-day swelling — return to Level 2; do not start running with a clear single-leg hop asymmetry |
| Level 4 running and sport-specific return | Level 3 passed; hopping and cutting pain-free | Straight easy jog first; no worsening 24 hours after running; one full training session completed; accelerations, cuts and hills pain-free; confidence in the ankle | Post-run pain, swelling or instability returns — return to Level 3; discomfort after a full training session needs clinical or rehabilitation review |

Timing references from the BJSM 2018 guideline: a distortion usually returns to full work and sport at 3–4 weeks; a partial or total ligament rupture mostly returns at 6–8 weeks depending on physiotherapy results; post-surgery return to demanding sport takes 12–16 weeks. AAOS puts minor sprains at about 2 weeks and more severe ones at 6–12 weeks; individual variation is large, so time is a reference and the pass criteria are the gate.

## Acute care: three positions reported side by side, not averaged

Sources do not fully agree on how to handle the first days after a sprain, and this page reports that divergence rather than picking a winner.

- **AAOS (OrthoInfo)**: home RICE — rest; ice 20–30 minutes, 3–4 times daily, with a cloth barrier and no direct skin contact; compression wrap or brace; elevate the ankle above heart level as often as possible in the first 48 hours.
- **NHS**: for the first 2–3 days, PRICE — protection, rest, ice up to 20 minutes every 2–3 hours, compression, elevation; avoid heat, alcohol and massage in the first couple of days; keep moving within pain once the area can be moved without pain stopping you, to avoid stiffness.
- **BJSM PEACE & LOVE editorial**: after 1–3 days of protection, rest should be minimised because prolonged rest compromises tissue strength and quality; elevation has weak evidence but a low risk-to-benefit ratio; it questions routine anti-inflammatory medication and ice, arguing that inhibiting inflammation may impair tissue repair; early pain-free loading, pain-free aerobic activity and exercise are the main rehabilitation line. The BJSM 2018 guideline states RICE is not advised as a treatment modality after a lateral ankle sprain, immobilisation should not be used, and functional support (brace preferred over tape) for 4–6 weeks is preferred together with an exercise programme.

Common ground that holds across sources: **brief protection, avoid re-injury, compression and elevation to settle swelling, early pain-free movement within range**. Whether and how long to ice, and whether to use anti-inflammatories, differ between sources; this page gives no medication dose. People with stomach, kidney, cardiovascular conditions or bleeding history must ask a clinician or pharmacist before anti-inflammatory medication.

## Red flags that warrant imaging

Whether to order an X-ray or MRI is a clinical decision. The following signals warrant prompt care where a professional decides on imaging:

- Tenderness over the dorsal side of one or both malleoli, tenderness at the base of the fifth metatarsal, tenderness of the navicular, or inability to walk at least four steps (Ottawa Ankle Rules, used within one week of trauma to rule out fracture; sensitivity 86%–99%, negative predictive value 97%–99%);
- A crack heard at injury, deformity, or an odd limb angle;
- Numbness, tingling or pins and needles in the foot or lower leg, or blue/grey/cold skin;
- Large or worsening swelling or bruising, or complete inability to bear weight beyond a few steps;
- Signs of a high ankle (syndesmosis) injury, or symptoms continuing beyond 6–8 weeks despite conservative treatment;
- Fever, local redness and heat, or chills, where infection must be excluded.

AAOS states an ankle sprain is largely a clinical diagnosis from history, symptoms and examination; MRI is not required to diagnose a sprain and is usually ordered to assess ligaments, cartilage or tendons, to check a suspected high ankle sprain, or when symptoms persist beyond 6–8 weeks despite conservative care. The PAASS consensus also excludes ligament structural integrity on imaging from the return-to-sport decision (89% agreement): clearance rests on functional performance, not how the ligament looks on a scan.

## Stop rules

- Sharp pain, giving way or clear instability during any exercise stops that movement immediately; step back one level.
- If pain or swelling has not returned to baseline 24 hours after running or exercise, do not add load; stay at the current level after two failed assessments.
- If an imaging red flag or systemic infection signal appears, stop the self-guided protocol and seek urgent or outpatient assessment.
- Do not force hopping or cutting because a brace or tape holds the ankle. Bracing reduces recurrence risk (BJSM 2018: brace or tape reduces recurrent sprains RR 0.30, 95% CI 0.21–0.43) but cannot replace strength and balance rehabilitation.
- Prior recurrent sprains, chronic instability, or dropping the strengthening work halfway are common causes of chronic instability; incomplete rehabilitation raises the chance of another sprain.

## Who does what

- **Runner**: record pain, swelling, weight-bearing and the response to each level; move up or back by the pass criteria, without skipping levels.
- **Doctor / emergency service**: decide whether there is a fracture, high ankle injury, or need for imaging; set the grade and whether immobilisation is required.
- **Physiotherapist**: design strength, range-of-motion and proprioception work, assess side-to-side symmetry and timing of return.
- **This site**: provide the framework, level gates and care boundaries; it does not diagnose, arrange imaging, give medication doses, or promise a recovery timeline.

## Common misuse

- **"Once it stops hurting I can go straight back to running."** Pain-free does not mean strength, balance and hopping capacity have recovered; straight jogging can re-sprain before Levels 2–3 symmetry passes.
- **"Stay in bed until all swelling goes."** Guidelines agree prolonged immobilisation brings stiffness and function loss; early pain-free movement is recommended, but not loading through pain.
- **"Brace it and skip the strength work."** Support is protection and recurrence prevention; the rehabilitation line is exercise. The BJSM 2018 guideline notes early exercise reduces recurrent sprains (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.51–0.76).
- **"A small single-leg hop asymmetry does not matter, finish the race first."** Hop, cutting and sport-specific asymmetry are recurrence signals; the PAASS consensus lists hopping, agility, sport-specific activities and completing a full session as return assessment items.
- **"Ice and anti-inflammatories after the sprain speed it up."** PEACE & LOVE and the BJSM 2018 guideline both reserve judgement on routine anti-inflammatories and ice; follow clinical advice on actual use. This page gives no dose.

## Scope and limits

This protocol is a rehabilitation education framework, not a diagnosis, grade, imaging order or medication prescription. A sprain can be accompanied by fracture, high ankle injury, tendon or cartilage problems. Children, pregnant runners, post-operative runners, people with underlying conditions or a history of recurrent sprains should be assessed clinically first. Level counts and symmetry standards are example gates and cannot replace an individualised physiotherapy assessment; if any red flag appears, seek care first.

## What would change this protocol

- **Grade and associated injuries**: partial or total ligament rupture, combined fracture or high ankle injury change the timeline and restrictions completely and follow a clinical plan.
- **Sport**: running, trail running and court/field cutting events make different demands on agility; Level 3–4 sport-specific content is replaced by the event.
- **Evidence updates**: ankle sprain guidelines, return-to-sport consensus and prevention research continue to evolve; later high-quality evidence shifts this protocol at review.
- **Individual background**: age, number of prior sprains, leg alignment and baseline strength change how long each level takes.
