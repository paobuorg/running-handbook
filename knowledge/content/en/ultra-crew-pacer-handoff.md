## What this page solves

Most hundred-mile logistics failures are not “not enough gels” but messy handoffs: crew cannot find the runner, the pacer grabs the wrong bag, the head torch is dead, the next section has no water, or the runner is hypothermic and nobody dares to call it. A handoff is not “meet and hand over”; it is a role, checklist and phrase protocol.

This page turns crew/pacer roles, the 10 minutes before, the 3-minute handoff, night handoffs, rule boundaries and withdrawal communication into an executable table. It does not replace the race rules; allowed points, kit and pacer rules differ by race, so read the official page first.

## Roles: who owns what

Crew has at least four roles: driver (route, parking, time), fuel (food, water, kit), medical (symptoms, medication, warmth), communications (race app, messages, emergency calls). One person doing all four will fail in the second half.

A pacer is not a “running companion”; it is a moving safety check: gait, clear speech, warmth and fuelling. Most races forbid a pacer from carrying the runner’s water, food, torch, shoes or clothing; the pacer reminds and accompanies.

## Rule boundaries: read the race page first

Western States: one pacer at a time from Foresthill (mile 62) to the finish; if leaving Michigan Bluff after 20:00, pacing may start there; changes only at Foresthill, Rucky Chucky north side, Green Gate, Pointed Rocks and Robie Point; pacer 18+.

Hardrock: in 2027 pacing starts at Grouse Gulch, with meetings at Ouray, Telluride, Chapman and KT if hiking in; runners over 60 may have a pacer for the whole run. UTMB: personal assistance only at designated aid stations, one person, bag no more than 30 litres; pacing outside spectator areas can be penalised.

## Ten minutes before handoff

Crew arrives first: park, set the table, lay out water, food, batteries, warmth and torch by bag; open the tracker and confirm the last checkpoint and ETA. Do not pile everything into one heap that cannot be found at the handoff.

Pacer self-checks first: torch on, spare batteries, warm layer, gloves, phone, race number/permit and personal fuel. A pacer does not arrive empty-handed; bring your own water and warmth.

## The three-minute handoff

Fixed order: ask “are you okay / cold / dizzy” → swap water and fill the next section → hand easy food → swap torch/batteries → add warmth → feet/trash → confirm next distance, climb, cut-off and escape point.

Do not hold a long review here. The runner needs four sentences: how far, how much climb, what cut-off, where next. A stop over three minutes cools the runner and blocks other crews.

## Night handoff

Night adds three items: primary/spare torch and batteries, warm layer, caffeine/hot water if allowed. Sit the runner, wrap a blanket, then change the torch; do not leave them standing and shivering while you search for batteries.

At night the pacer watches speech, finger dexterity and gait. If hypothermia appears: warm, get indoors at the checkpoint, call medical. Do not soothe with “you’ll warm up in a bit”.

## How to pack the handoff bags

One clear bag per checkpoint, labelled with checkpoint, kilometre and runner name. Inside, three small bags: water/electrolytes, food, warmth/torch/batteries. Add an A5 card: next distance, climb, cut-off, escape point and crew phone.

Do not mix all checkpoints in one big box. On race day you may handle several runners and crews; clear bags and labels are the only way to find anything in 30 seconds.

## Pacer pacing and talk

A pacer does not “pull” the runner faster; the job is to hold effort: cap climbs, warn on descents, fuel on flats. Match cadence and do not lead too fast.

Use concrete language: “right turn in 200 m”, “next checkpoint 4.2 km, 380 m climb”, “it is 03:10, cut-off 05:00”. Do not ask “how do you feel”; ask “dizzy? cold? can you eat?”.

## Withdrawal communication

When red flags appear, the pacer and crew goal is not “talk them into continuing” but a safe exit. Stop, warm, reach the checkpoint/medical, then decide. Do not debate on the trail.

After withdrawal, crew handles transport, extended lodging, kit recovery, race notification and medical observation. The pacer gets the runner to the escape point and does not split mid-trail.

## Common mistakes

Mistake one: crew holding a loud review at the checkpoint while the runner cools. Two: pacer carrying the runner’s water/food (often illegal). Three: mixing all checkpoint bags. Four: night with only a primary torch and no spare batteries. Five: debating withdrawal mid-trail.

Six: pacer running their own pace. Seven: no A5 card and relying on memory for the next section. Each turns logistics into an incident.

## Chinese runner’s perspective

In Chinese races, crew often rely on self-driving and WeChat groups; parking may be a long walk from the checkpoint. Recon the parking-to-checkpoint time and do not arrive five minutes before the runner.

For overseas races, solve language, SIM, rental car and insurance; put rescue number, hotel address and pacer permit on a bilingual card. Crew is not one person doing translator, driver and cook; rotate at least two people.

## Checklist

Pre-race: rules, allowed points, pacer permit, checkpoint bags, A5 cards, tracker app, spare batteries, warmth, first-aid kit, fuel/parking plan. Handoff: state → water → food → torch → warmth → feet → four next-section sentences.

Post-race: kit recovery, medical observation, withdrawal report, photos/notes. Tape the checklist in the car; do not rely on memory.
