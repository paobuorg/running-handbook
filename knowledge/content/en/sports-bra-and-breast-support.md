## Definitions and scope

The core job of a sports bra is not shaping but reducing breast displacement and discomfort during running. It applies to every cup size, not only large breasts. Whether you need high support depends on breast size, how running feels and how well the bra fits, not on a label saying "high impact".

This page covers support and fit during exercise. It does not diagnose breast disease or evaluate a bra as a medical treatment. A new lump, skin change or unusual nipple discharge should go through a medical pathway first, not a bra shopping trip.

## Why breasts move during running

Breasts do not have enough internal anatomy to hold themselves in place. Skin and fine Cooper's ligaments are the main suspensory structures, so breast tissue can move relatively freely over the chest wall; repeated vertical trunk motion during running amplifies that movement.

Two commonly cited measurements frame the problem:

- For some women, **as little as 2 cm of vertical displacement is enough to cause breast discomfort**.
- Women with large breasts can experience **more than 10 cm of vertical displacement** during running without adequate support.

Both come from biomechanics research and are not predictions for every individual. They show that support is not a minor comfort issue; it directly changes how running feels.

## How much support changes: two displacement studies

| Study | Sample | Condition | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jamkrajang & Limroongreungrat (2015) | 30 female collegiate athletes, B75–C70 | Treadmill at 9 km/h, no bra / encapsulation / compression | Vertical displacement during running: no bra 42.6 / 48.8 mm, encapsulation 23.3 / 22.8 mm, compression 18.9 / 19.1 mm (right/left) |
| Same study | Same | Jogging at 6 km/h | Vertical displacement reduced by 39.4% right / 36.4% left with the encapsulation bra and 55.8% right / 47.5% left with the compression bra; only the compression bra's right-side figure exceeded 50% |
| Fong & Powell (2022) | 15 recreational runners, B–DD cup | 10-minute treadmill run, low versus high support | Relative oxygen consumption 30.9 to 28.7 ml/kg/min; running economy 88.6 to 95.2 m/L O2 |

In Jamkrajang's study, the compression bra produced significantly less vertical displacement than the encapsulation bra during running (p < 0.05), while the encapsulation bra allowed more independent motion of each breast. The sample was only 30 women with B75–C70 sizes, so it does not generalise to every body.

Fong & Powell was even smaller, with 15 women, but measured both oxygen consumption and running economy. With high support, relative oxygen consumption fell by about 7% and running economy rose by about 7%; cadence, step length and ground contact time did not change significantly (p = 0.149, 0.300 and 0.151). The change in running economy correlated with breast size (r = 0.807, p < 0.001). The authors also note that a previous study of only 10 participants (Risius et al., 2017) found no significant changes with greater support.

The safer conclusion: **support may produce a small economy benefit, but the reliable gain is less displacement and discomfort. Do not expect a new bra to make you markedly faster.** Both studies are small and tested specific bra models.

## Fit matters more than the support label

Steele's 2015 review reports an uncomfortable figure: across studies, **up to 100% of women were wearing the wrong bra size**. Poor fit is linked not only to discomfort but also to upper-limb neural symptoms, deep furrows from strap pressure, non-cyclical breast pain, and neck and back pain.

The same review offers several practical observations:

- In a survey of 267 Australian women (mean age 27.2), only 41% wore an encapsulating sports bra during physical activity;
- The most disliked features were shoulder straps cutting in and straps slipping off;
- Moving straps from a traditional vertical alignment to a crossed-back design reduced slipping without reducing overall support;
- Commercially available strap cushions did not reduce strap pressure in the cited study;
- A correctly fitted sports bra did not significantly affect maximal exercise performance or respiratory function during submaximal exercise.

Use this three-step fitting test rather than relying on a "high support / low support" label:

1. **Measure the underband first:** measure the ribcage snugly; the band should sit close without digging in. You should still breathe normally with two fingers fitting under it.
2. **Then check straps and cups:** straps should not slip or cut deep furrows; the cups should contain the whole breast, with no spill at the armpit or lower edge and no centre gapping.
3. **Finally do a movement test:** run in place for 30 seconds, take several deep breaths and raise your arms; if a strap slips, the band rides up, or breathing is restricted, change size or construction rather than padding the problem.

"Tighter" is not "better". An over-tight underband and straps transfer pressure to the ribs, shoulders and skin, creating a new problem while solving another.

### Fitting checklist

The table below is an editorial fitting routine based on those fit issues, not a research finding:

| Check | Good fit | Common problem |
|---|---|---|
| Underband | Sits horizontally on the ribs and does not ride up when the arms are raised | Too loose, so support comes mainly from the straps |
| Cup | Contains the breast tissue without obvious spillage or flattening | Too small, pressing tissue out or compressing the chest |
| Straps | Stay on the shoulders without digging or slipping | Too thin or too long; a crossed back may reduce slipping |
| Skin | No persistent redness, chafing or stinging after running | Seams, labels or band edges rubbing |
| Dynamic test | Jog, jump and raise the arms; displacement feels controlled and pain-free | Trying it only while standing still |

If you can do only one thing in the shop, do the dynamic test: jog on the spot for 30 seconds and then jump a few times. Static fit is not running fit.

## Compression versus encapsulation

- **Compression** holds the breasts against the chest as one unit, usually without separate cups; in Jamkrajang's sample it produced the smallest vertical displacement and was more symmetrical left to right.
- **Encapsulation** gives each breast its own space and allows more independent motion; displacement was slightly greater in the same study, but some runners prefer the separation.

This is not a hierarchy. Compression may suit someone who wants maximum restriction; encapsulation may suit someone who dislikes being flattened. The final test is cup fit, strap stability and pain during running. Neither study is large enough to prescribe a bra type by cup size.

## Pain boundaries: cyclical versus needs review

The NHS describes period-related breast pain as starting up to two weeks before a period, worsening and then resolving when the period ends; it feels dull, heavy or aching, usually affects both breasts and sometimes spreads to the armpit. Other causes include injuries or sprains to the neck, shoulder or back, the contraceptive pill and some antidepressants, mastitis or a breast abscess, pregnancy, and hormonal changes during menopause.

The NHS states clearly that **breast pain by itself is unlikely to be a symptom of cancer.** That does not mean every breast symptom can be watched at home. The following follow NHS urgent pathways:

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Breast pain with a very high temperature or feeling hot and shivery, or a breast that is red, hot or swollen | See a GP promptly or contact NHS 111 |
| A hard lump that does not move around | Urgent appointment |
| Nipple discharge, which may be streaked with blood | Urgent appointment |
| A change in breast shape | Urgent appointment |
| Skin dimpling like orange peel | Urgent appointment |
| A rash on or around the nipple, or a nipple that has sunk into the breast | Urgent appointment |

For ordinary breast pain, the NHS suggests a properly fitted bra during the day and a soft bra to sleep in. It also notes that there is little evidence that vitamin E tablets or evening primrose oil help. The NHS page mentions painkillers, but this page gives no medication advice; if you need pain relief, follow a doctor's or pharmacist's guidance.

## Specific populations

In a survey of 74 breast cancer survivors, 70.3% reported bra discomfort during exercise, and "I can't find a bra that is comfortable to exercise in" ranked fourth out of 34 perceived barriers. In a later online survey of 482 survivors, not being able to find a comfortable bra was one of the top three barriers. If you are in treatment or after surgery, bra choice may depend on the type of surgery, lymphoedema risk and scar sensitivity. Discuss it with your treating team or a specialist bra fitter rather than applying general runner advice.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Tighter means more support"**: support comes from a fitted underband, cup structure and strap distribution, not simple compression. Over-tightening can compress ribs and shoulders and create new pain.
- **"Small breasts do not need a sports bra"**: 2 cm of displacement is enough to cause discomfort in some women; need depends on personal sensation, not the cup label.
- **"Expensive bras always fit"**: price is not fit. Underband and cup depth vary widely between brands, so try before buying.
- **"One bra lasts until it falls apart"**: elastic degrades with use. Replace it when the underband is clearly loose, straps slip frequently, or the cup no longer contains the tissue; there is no universal lifespan because it depends on frequency and washing.
- **"Breast pain means cancer"**: the NHS says breast pain alone is unlikely to be cancer, but a hard lump, bloody discharge, shape change, orange-peel skin or an inverted nipple needs urgent assessment.
- **"A new bra treats breast pain"**: a well-fitted bra is one comfort measure, not a diagnosis or treatment. Persistent, non-cyclical pain or any red flag should be reviewed.

### One sizing example

Example (hypothetical): underband 76 cm and bust 90 cm gives a 14 cm difference, which falls between B and C on many charts; because brands differ, the deciding test is no displacement, no pressure and no rubbing during the jump test.
