## What this page solves

Western States qualification has three gates: a race on the official qualifying list, a finish inside the qualifying window, and a lottery application in November.

The practical test is simple: can you name the exact qualifying race, the exact result, the date it was run and the official page that lists it? If any answer is “I think so”, the entry is not ready.

## Qualification structure

The official list is updated each September. Only listed races count; virtual events are not accepted. Distance or climb alone does not make a qualifier.

Write the structure on one page: what you must finish, where it must sit in the calendar, and which body confirms it. This prevents a late discovery that a race was never on the list.

## Which races qualify

The 2027 qualifying window is 3 November 2025 to 8 November 2026. A finish outside that window, or after the race cut-off, does not count.

Choose qualifiers by evidence, not by prestige. Check the official list, the cut-off, the surface and the travel cost; a convenient qualifier you finish beats a famous one you miss.

## Standards and category

There is no Boston-style age-grade time table. The gate is a listed qualifier finished inside its cut-off; age only sets the 18+ entry minimum.

Standards change by age, gender, category or year. Copy the current official table into your own note, with the access date, and delete last year’s version from your phone.

## Entry timeline

For 2027, lottery registration ran 1–21 November 2026 and the draw is 5 December 2026. If you read this on 26 November, the 2027 window is closed.

Build a backward calendar from the race date: qualifier, entry window, document check, travel and taper. Put each deadline in a calendar with a two-week warning.

## Common mistakes

The common errors are using an unlisted race, finishing after the cut-off, and treating the lottery as first-come-first-served. It is a window, not a queue.

Most rejections are administrative, not athletic: wrong race, wrong window, missing proof, missed payment. Read the checklist before blaming fitness.

## From qualification to start line

After selection you submit the qualifier, pay, complete race briefing and collect your bib. The 2027 guide, not the 2026 guide, sets start and crew rules.

After selection, the work shifts to proof, payment, travel and kit. The start line is not the finish of the qualification process; it is the start of execution.

## Cost, travel and places

Budget entry, flights to Reno or Sacramento, start/finish beds and a crew vehicle. The race does not shuttle runners back to Olympic Valley after Auburn.

Put every cost in one sheet: entry, travel, bed, kit, insurance and a buffer. If the total is uncomfortable, choose a closer qualifier rather than borrowing for a race.

## If you miss the cut

If you miss, keep accumulating lottery tickets and check that your qualifier remains inside the window. Do not run multiple 100-milers just to “buy” another ticket.

If you miss, separate the cause: eligibility, performance, or lottery. Each has a different fix. Repeating the same entry without changing the cause is the expensive path.

## Training and proof

Train for long climbs, long descents and heat. A qualifier is a ticket, not the end of preparation; rehearse night, crew handoffs and foot care.

Train the demands of the qualifier and the target race, not a generic plan. A qualifier is evidence; the target race is the performance you actually want.

## A 12-month checklist

Twelve months out, choose the qualifier and check the official list. Nine months out, build the base and book a refundable room. Six months out, run a tune-up and confirm the entry window. Three months out, submit documents and book travel. Six weeks out, simulate the race. Two weeks out, taper and re-read the rules.

Put every deadline in a calendar with a two-week warning. The most common failure is not fitness but a document that was never uploaded, a payment that was never made, or a race that was never on the list.

## Chinese runner’s perspective

For a runner based in China, the binding constraint is often travel time and visa processing, not the qualifying standard. Start the visa and flight plan as soon as the entry window is known, and keep a refundable bed until the draw.

If the race is in the US or Europe, plan arrival two to three days before packet pickup, not the night before. A delayed flight is a qualification problem only if you let it become one.

## Three filters for choosing a qualifier

Western States qualification is not “finish any 100K”. Filter by distance and category, then by the official qualifying-race list, then by lottery and travel. Fail one layer and the finish does not become a lottery entry.

| Filter | Confirm | Consequence of failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance/category | An accepted 100K or 100M finish | Result not recognised |
| Official list | The current Qualifying Races list | A “similar distance” race that does not count |
| Proof | Official result, timing, within cut-off | Materials rejected |
| Lottery entry | Submitted in window, details consistent | Missed lottery |

The hardest qualifier is not automatically the best. Choose a race you can finish comfortably within the cut-off, travel to sensibly and recover from; a famous race that leaves you injured is a worse investment.

## Twelve months after a miss

Separate a miss into ineligible, incomplete materials, or lottery luck. Ineligible: choose a listed race. Incomplete: obtain official results and proof. Lottery only: retain eligibility and keep training. Do not buy a transfer or believe in an “internal draw”.

For the next cycle, finish the qualifier 12–18 months before the lottery, leaving room for recovery and a backup race; do not run the first 100K two months before the draw.
