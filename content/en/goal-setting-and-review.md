## Definition and preconditions

Goal setting is defined as turning “run better” into observable standards; review compares plan with actual at fixed intervals. The page applies to runners with a race or cycle who arrange their own load. It holds when the goal is your own, a checkable record exists each week, and you will revise a goal the evidence no longer supports. A goal never revisited is only a wish.

## Outcome, performance and process goals

Outcome goals point to a finish, placing or time. Performance goals point to your pace, distance or technique. Process goals point to weekly actions. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that goal setting improved task performance, with effects differing by goal type (process goals d = 1.36, performance goals d = 0.44, outcome goals d = 0.09); specific and non-specific goals did not differ significantly (d = 0.37 vs 0.72). The abstract did not report moderation by proximity, experience or feedback; performance findings were mixed, and cross-study evidence for some psychological outcomes was limited. Outcome goals depend on weather, course, competitors and daily condition; process goals are closer to control.

## Proximity: weekly and 12-week goals

A weekly goal gives fast feedback; a 12-week goal sets direction. Use three layers: a season goal (a race in 12 weeks), a block goal (one focus every 3 to 4 weeks) and a weekly goal (runs, length, strength day). Weekly goals must add up to the block goal.

## How to do it: three steps

1. Write one outcome goal with a date and distance, such as “finish a half marathon (21.1 km) in 12 weeks”, plus two limits: no running through pain, sleep on most nights.
2. Translate it into process goals with frequency or duration: “run 4 times a week, including 1 long run and 1 strength session”, not “run more”.
3. Fix a 5 to 10 minute review on the same day weekly, compare log with plan, and write the decision into next week's plan.

## Feedback and the log

Record date, distance, duration, intensity, feel, pain and sleep. The same fields and timing each week keep the record comparable; a watch cannot explain pain.

| Goal type | Example | Controllability | Review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | A target time in 12 weeks | Low | Every 4 weeks |
| Performance | Easy pace within a band | Medium | Every 2 weeks |
| Process | 4 runs; 2 strength sessions | High | Weekly |

## Worked example: a 12-week half marathon

The numbers are hypothetical. Assume a half marathon (21.1 km) in 12 weeks, 3 runs weekly for about 20 km, longest run 8 km. The CDC baseline for adults is at least 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly plus strength on 2 days. The process goals become 4 runs weekly, 3 easy and 1 long; the long run starts at 8 km and grows 2 km every 2 weeks; strength twice weekly for 20 minutes; no increase in the final 3 weeks.

## Monthly review questions

- Is completion higher or lower than last month?
- Are fatigue, sleep or pain trending worse?
- Is the block goal still realistic, or must date, distance or course change?

## Common misconceptions

- A hidden outcome goal. “Run at a certain pace” says nothing about frequency or duration.
- Adding training after two missed weeks. Find the cause first: load, sleep, work or motivation.
- Treating review as a report card. Review the plan, not willpower.

## When to stop or seek care

Pause the plan if pain worsens or walking is affected, if persistent fatigue comes with a decline or plateau in performance, or if sleep, mood or control around training deteriorates. Chest pain, fainting, unusual palpitations or severe breathlessness needs emergency care, not next week's review.

## Frequency, review and progression

Review weekly for 5 to 10 minutes, hold a 20-minute monthly review every 4 weeks, and reset the goal every 12 weeks. If completion is below half for 2 consecutive weeks, cut next week's volume by about 10% and restore completion before adding load.

## Weekly checklist

- Write process goals with counts, distance or duration.
- Put the review slot in the calendar.
- Record sleep, pain and fatigue.
- Check that weekly goals match the block goal.

## Scope and limits

This page does not provide an individual plan or guarantee a result, and does not replace medical assessment or coaching. People with a chronic condition, injury history, pregnancy or disordered-eating history should discuss goals with a professional.
