## Definition and scope

Route safety means turning route, weather, company, supplies and an escape plan into an executable plan before leaving, not just a GPX. It applies to road, night and trail runs, when someone knows your route and return time, you will shorten the plan and kit matches the weather.

## Tell someone not going

The NPS recommends giving a trusted person who is not on the trip the route, contacts, expected arrival and return times and companions. Keep an offline map and backup contacts; without coverage, consider a locator beacon and conserve battery. State the route and return time, and update if it changes.

## The water half rule

The NPS advises carrying enough water for the conditions and turning back before half is gone. The rule replaces "I can manage" with a measurable signal: remaining volume. Carry for the hottest, slowest, longest version of the day and do not assume a refill point is open.

## Heat and cold

Heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, headache and fainting in heat can indicate heat exhaustion; with vomiting or worsening symptoms, cool the person and seek help. Confusion, slurred speech, very high body temperature, a rapid strong pulse or loss of consciousness suggest heat stroke: call first and keep cooling. Extreme cold with wind creates dangerous wind-chill; exposed skin and extremities are most prone to frostbite, and hypothermia occurs when heat loss outpaces production.

## Storms and flash floods

Check warnings, prepare for lightning, rain and flooding, and keep a Plan B. When storms approach, leave ridges, open ground and isolated trees; do not keep climbing to finish a loop. Flash floods can arrive after rain fell elsewhere, and gullies are the worst place to wait.

## Worked example

Figures are hypothetical: 10 km out and back, turn point at 5 km, 600 ml of water at 6:00 per kilometre, about 30 minutes each way. Under the half rule, turn when 300 ml remains. Assume 250 ml is left at 5 km, so 350 ml has been drunk, beyond the share that allows progress; turn immediately.

## Conditions and actions

| Condition | Action | Do not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thunder or building storms | Descend, leave exposure | Stay on a ridge |
| Discoloured stream, upstream noise | Move out, go higher | Wait in the channel |
| Sweating, dizziness, nausea | Stop, shade, cool, get help | Let pace override symptoms |
| Confusion, slurred speech | Call emergency services, cool | Send them home alone |

## How to: three steps

1. Send the route, return time and companions to someone not going, and agree a check-in time.
2. Carry water and kit for the weather, write the turn and escape points, and make "half the water left" a firm signal.
3. At the turn point, check water, energy, weather and elapsed time; shorten if any is off, and check in afterwards.

## Common misconceptions

- Treating a GPX as proof of safety; it describes geometry, not closures or today's weather.
- Thinking a charged phone is enough; without coverage, map, compass, headlamp and whistle are the backup.
- Treating the turn point as a target, not a checkpoint; reaching it without deciding is no checkpoint at all.

## When to stop or seek help

Stop with heat exhaustion that persists or worsens, vomiting, altered consciousness, inability to stand, chest pain, severe breathlessness, pale numb skin, or approaching storms or floods. Heat stroke, loss of consciousness or inability to walk are emergencies: call emergency services.

## Running alone and harassment: prevention, escape, reporting

Risk on a solo run comes from other people as well as traffic and weather. This section covers prevention, escape and reporting only. It **does not judge an individual case and does not recommend self-defence moves or weapons**.

**Before you go.** The Road Runners Club of America advises runners to stay alert, trust their intuition and avoid a person or area that feels unsafe, and to vary routes and times so they are less predictable; it also advises running against traffic and obeying signals. Add three habits: keep one ear free of headphones, share live location with one trusted contact, and at night choose lit routes with many exits (see [night running and traffic](https://learn.paobu.org/en/articles/night-running-and-traffic/)).

**During an incident: escape first.** You do not have to prove intent or argue. Move towards people, light and cameras; go into a shop, guard post or lobby, then call. In mainland China the police number is **110**; public police guidance is to get away first and then report, describing location, number of people, appearance and direction of escape. The same applies when the person is an acquaintance, coach or teammate.

**Preserve evidence.** Note time, place, appearance and words; keep messages, screenshots and witness contacts; record audio or video only where local law permits. Do not delete records because "it was nothing".

**Where to report.** At a race or club: the organiser, club lead or welfare/safeguarding officer. UK Athletics' 2023 adult safeguarding policy and regulations include referral to statutory safeguarding agencies as part of the process, a useful reference for the principle that sports organisations need a clear reporting route. Legally, Article 23 of China's Law on the Protection of Women's Rights and Interests prohibits sexual harassment by words, text, images or physical conduct and provides complaint and handling mechanisms. Support: the All-China Women's Federation **12338** hotline offers advice and referral. Internationally, the IOC 2016 consensus defines harassment and abuse as non-accidental violence (psychological, physical, sexual and neglect) and places prevention and response duties on sports organisations.

**What this page does not do:** it does not decide whether a specific act is unlawful, does not recommend self-defence moves or weapons, does not generalise about neighbourhood safety, and does not quote unverified incidence rates. Harassment prevalence among runners in China, city-level police "running safety" pages and a Chinese athletics safeguarding page are **unknown**.

## Frequency and progression

Spend a minute on plan and forecast before outings, review closures weekly, and refresh kit and escape plans each season. Extend distance only after three outings on the same route finished on time with water left.

## Checklist

- Route, return time and companions sent.
- Water carried for the hottest case; turn point written down.
- Headlamp, spare batteries, whistle, first aid and warmth packed.
- Offline and physical maps plus compass usable.

## Scope and limits

This page does not provide live closure, rescue, access authorisation or medical advice for a route. Land-manager rules differ; emergency numbers vary.
